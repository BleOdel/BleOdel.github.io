Horizon Europe · University of Greenwich, 2023–2024

SUN: Cyber Security for Human-Centred XR

How can immersive security research support healthcare, accessibility and industrial scenarios?

SUN (Social & human-centred XR) is a multi-national Horizon Europe consortium developing secure, trustworthy XR for rehabilitation, worker safety, and accessibility, including for people with severe mobility and communication impairments.

From PhD research to reusable tooling

As XR Software Developer, I extended the multi-user Unity/OpenXR security testbed from my PhD research to support cross-partner applications, instrumented for security monitoring and threat experimentation. I designed a real-time intrusion detection system monitoring orientation, gaze, and system/network telemetry, intended to flag anomalous behaviour including previously unseen threats in XR use cases, reusing the core detection approach from Telemetry-Driven XR Intrusion Detection.

Into the project’s demonstrators

I built automated telemetry aggregation pipelines across multiple XR endpoints, and worked with the research team to integrate the XR security monitoring tool into the project’s demonstrators. In-headset threat-visualisation mechanisms were designed to deliver safety alerts while keeping disruption to the task and to immersion low; these were demonstrator integrations rather than production deployments.

Continuity beyond the contract

After my formal contract ended, I continued supporting the SUN consortium by providing the core IDS architecture and facilitating technical reviews with partner teams, so the security tooling and experimental frameworks from my PhD research stayed available to the project.
