VR Cybersecurity Taxonomy

How should researchers systematically reason about security and privacy threats in immersive environments?

The first systematic classification of cybersecurity challenges in Virtual Reality Environments (VREs), mapping what aspect of the system may be exploited, what security property may be breached, what impact a breach has on the VR experience, and what damage the attacker intends to cause.

Published: Odeleye, Loukas, Heartfield, Sakellari, Panaousis, Spyridonis — Computers & Security, 2023 Role: Lead author, PhD research

The problem

Although virtual reality is not a new technology, its adoption across sectors beyond entertainment has outpaced the security research community's attention to the new cyber threats it introduces. VR's variety of system components presents an extensive attack surface, while its emphasis on immersion and presence means an attack can target the user directly — yet a head-mounted display prevents the user from observing that attack's impact on their immediate physical environment.

The taxonomy

The taxonomy answers four questions about any VR cyber threat. Exploit — what aspect of the system may be exploited: its system parameters (network, display, audio, sensors) or the user's human sensory stimulus. Breach — what security property is broken: confidentiality, integrity or availability. Impact — what effect the breach has on the VR experience: interaction, immersion or presence. Intent — whether the intended damage is physical or non-physical.

Applying this taxonomy to existing published attacks and defences showed that current VR security research is heavily weighted toward authentication (70% of surveyed defences) and confidentiality breaches, leaving intrusion detection and availability-focused defences comparatively unexplored — the gap this doctoral research addresses directly (see Telemetry-Driven XR Intrusion Detection).

Why it matters

A shared taxonomy gives researchers from different backgrounds — systems engineers, HCI researchers, security analysts — a common vocabulary for identifying where their work can contribute to protecting immersive environments, from understanding an attack's impact to designing new defences.
