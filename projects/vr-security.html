PhD research · University of Greenwich, 2019–2025

Detecting Cybersecurity Threats in Virtual Reality

Can a cyberattack against VR be detected from the behaviour of the system itself?

Doctoral research combining a custom-built VR testbed, a novel GPU-based cyber attack, and an unsupervised machine-learning intrusion detection system: a step toward reactive VR security defences for cases where prevention alone is insufficient.

Thesis: Addressing Cyber Security Threats in Virtual Reality Environments (2024) Supervisors: Prof. Georgios Loukas, Dr Georgia Sakellari, Dr Ryan Heartfield

VR security testbed demo

The same room-scale testbed under normal conditions and during a controlled GPU-based attack. Both clips are silent and encoded at identical settings, so what differs between them is the attack rather than the compression.

What I built

I developed a modular VR research testbed in Unity using OpenXR and XR Interaction Toolkit. It combined locomotion, object interaction, avatar selection, a training lobby and experimental scenes. The testbed supported controlled evaluation of GPU-based attacks, collection of system telemetry and assessment of users’ responses to security warnings.

The problem

VR security research had concentrated on prevention, through risk assessment and authentication, which does not address what happens when prevention fails or a threat is new. Head-mounted displays compound the problem: they isolate users from real-world cues (network state, CPU usage, unfamiliar processes) that would normally hint at a compromise.

Four contributions

New attack→ Modular testbed→ Practical IDS→ Security taxonomy

A GPU-based attack that induces VR sickness on demand by manipulating frame rate; a custom-built, modular VR testbed designed specifically to carry out that attack and observe user reactions to real-time warnings; an Isolation Forest-based intrusion detection system designed to warn the user before an attack’s impact escalates (in the literature the thesis reviewed, the first VR intrusion detection system against VR cyber attacks); and a systematic taxonomy of VR cybersecurity challenges (see VR Cybersecurity Taxonomy) that helped identify where this reactive-defence gap sat in the field.

Collaborative capability

The testbed also included multiplayer functionality and a two-user coordination scene, as a basis for future studies of collaborative VR security. These capabilities extended the platform beyond the single-user experiment reported in the thesis; the collaborative scene was not evaluated in that study.

Where it led

The detection pipeline and participant study are reported in detail in Telemetry-Driven XR Intrusion Detection and Cyber-Physical Impact of VR Attacks. The work also informed the Horizon Europe SUN project, where I worked with the research team to integrate the XR security monitoring tool into its demonstrators.
